Neutrinos and the ways to see them

We study how neutrinos change their flavours, how they interact with us—or, more precisely, with the building blocks of us—and how they meddle in the dark sector.

We focus on developing original methods to detect and understand neutrinos, within a global network that transcends boundaries.

Research programme

Oscillation

The three masses and three flavours of neutrinos—exactly three, no more, no less—make flavour mixing and oscillation remarkably rich. Mass ordering and CP violation are among the major open questions about the mass and flavour structures of neutrinos. The VISOS project provides an intuitive visualisation of neutrino oscillations, giving “life” to neutrinos and allowing us to see how they behave under different circumstances.

Interaction

In the high-energy sector of neutrino oscillations, the neutrino flux and energy are reconstructed from the post-impact trails, which resemble the collision aftermath of a bowling game or a game of billiards. We know that atoms are small, but if an atom were the size of the Las Vegas Sphere, this neutrino impact area would be comparable to the tip of a ballpoint pen. No microscope is powerful enough to inspect the impact zone. The TKI methods allow what happens inside the nucleus not to stay inside the nucleus—the underlying dynamics are projected out, almost like a movie, via correlations among the post-impact trails observed in the detector.

JUNO & PandaX

Neutrinos are omnipresent. High-energy ones produced in the atmosphere are the less controlled cousins of those produced by accelerators, but always available and less costly. The low-energy ones from the sun would, if not properly studied, drown out signals from dark matter. Can we harvest these gifts of nature? We participate in experiments in China—JUNO, with a detector the size of the Royal Albert Hall, and PandaX, deep inside a mountain with 2400 metres of rock above—to answer this question.

Future detectors and beams

Active-target gas time projection chambers (TPCs) could provide the ultimate measurements of neutrino interactions needed for future neutrino oscillation measurements. Their development requires optimisation of the gas mixture, pressure, electric fields, and readout configuration. Warwick TPC (WarTPC) is an R&D platform for neutrino physics and beyond. Alongside detector development, novel neutrino beam concepts await opportunities for their realisation.
