Our programme combines original analysis techniques, precision neutrino measurements, event generators and advanced tracking detectors. We connect what can be learned from observed particles to how future experiments should be designed.

02 — JUNO

Atmospheric neutrinos at JUNO

A new window on neutrino mass and mixing. Our programme connects atmospheric-neutrino measurements, nuclear models and rare-event searches. I founded JUNO’s first UK group and GANYMEDE, its working group on GeV-neutrino interactions and nuclear effects. The name combines “GeV ν-A high-eNergY MEDium Effect” with a celestial connection: Ganymede is a moon of Jupiter, the mythological husband of Juno.

03 — PANDAX

Neutrinos at the dark-matter frontier

When a discovery signal becomes a background. Neutrinos can make xenon nuclei recoil, mimicking the signals sought in dark-matter searches. Our programme studies these backgrounds—from solar to atmospheric neutrinos—to understand what sets the sensitivity of future searches.

I established and lead Warwick’s PandaX-20T group, among its first international teams, participating as a Technical Collaborator.

04 — FUTURE DETECTORS

WarTPC: a platform for neutrino and rare-event physics

From new measurement concepts to detector capability. WarTPC develops low- and high-pressure gas tracking for neutrino and rare-event physics, combining hydrogen-rich targets, sensitivity to low-energy particles, and optical and charge readout. Building on the AIDAinnova prototype deliverable—a milestone for our programme—our ongoing programme advances gas-mixture studies, readout and reconstruction for future measurements.

05 — FUTURE BEAMS

nuSTORM: precision from stored muons

Better-known beams for more incisive measurements. nuSTORM would use stored-muon decays to deliver electron- and muon-flavour neutrino beams with precisely known fluxes. This offers a route to interaction measurements that support DUNE and Hyper-K, alongside searches for new physics.

I coordinate the core physics studies, connecting precision measurements, detector concepts and international strategy.

Independent detector development

WarTPC: making a new measurement possible

Why should nuclear effects always be part of a neutrino measurement?

Hydrogen offers an unusually clean target for neutrino interaction studies, but identifying hydrogen events demands experimental innovation. WarTPC is our high-pressure gaseous time projection chamber R&D platform at Warwick, linking this physics motivation to detector design, instrumentation and reconstruction.

Our work includes gas-mixture studies, charge and optical readout, detector commissioning and detailed simulation. The programme draws on leadership of the AIDAinnova high-pressure gaseous TPC task.

From concept to the laboratory

  • Hydrogen-rich gas targets and low-threshold tracking
  • Optical and charge readout development
  • THGEM and Timepix-based instrumentation
  • Pressure, gas composition and response studies
  • Simulation and track reconstruction

This is an active R&D platform, not a claim that all proposed neutrino–hydrogen measurements have already been demonstrated.

The research group · 课题组

A group connecting ideas, data and hardware

Students and researchers work on independent scientific problems within international experimental collaborations, with hands-on opportunities in detector development, computational modelling and physics analysis.

Research areas and training

Projects range from JUNO atmospheric-neutrino studies, T2K and SBND interaction measurements, and PandaX neutrino-background questions to WarTPC instrumentation, neutrino event generators and future beams. Group members can develop a full chain from a physics idea through simulation or measurement to a publication.

We value scientific independence, critical discussion and the ability to cross boundaries between hardware, software and physics.
