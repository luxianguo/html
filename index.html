Research programme

Understanding neutrinos. Opening new ways to explore.

Neutrinos have mass, yet its origin and ordering remain unresolved; their behaviour may also help explain why matter survived over antimatter. Dark matter shapes galaxies, but its particle identity remains unknown. These are questions about what the Universe is made of—and how it came to be.

Answering them requires more than larger experiments. We need to understand how neutrinos interact with matter, distinguish faint signals from backgrounds, and measure what present instruments cannot. Our group connects new analysis methods and experimental measurements with detectors that reveal more of each interaction and precision beams that let us interpret it reliably.

01 — OSCILLATIONS

VISOS: seeing flavour change

Making quantum behaviour visible. VISualisation of OScillation: three neutrino-flavour probabilities become a point in a triangle; their evolution traces a path. turns neutrino flavour change into geometric trajectories. I conceived VISOS and have led its development. Its interactive companion lets you explore how CP violation, matter and energy shape oscillations, connecting physical intuition with experimental questions.

The research group

Shared questions. Independent ideas.

We study neutrinos and dark matter, and develop new ways to investigate them. Our work brings together physics analysis, simulation and detector development, with students pursuing their own questions as part of a wider international research community.

Life in the group

The physics often takes us between data, calculations and the laboratory. A question arising in JUNO or PandaX may call for a closer look at neutrino interactions; a new measurement idea may lead to a test in WarTPC. Projects in T2K, DUNE and future beams offer further connections, with regular discussion helping ideas develop across the group.

Questions worth asking

What are we thinking about?

What can neutrino interactions reveal about the nucleus?

Reading correlations between outgoing particles to disentangle nuclear motion and reaction dynamics—and understand how they shape what we measure.

Can we measure neutrino interactions without nuclear effects?

Isolating interactions on hydrogen to study the underlying process without the complications of a many-nucleon nucleus.

What would a precision neutrino beam make possible?

Using stored muons to provide precisely known beams, opening measurements that conventional sources cannot readily deliver.

What could JUNO reveal over thirty years, beyond reactor neutrinos?

Looking to atmospheric and astrophysical neutrinos, and rare processes, to explore the wider physics reach of a long-running observatory.

How do we find dark matter within the neutrino fog?

Understanding neutrino backgrounds as they emerge, then using additional information—including recoil directions—to distinguish dark matter as those backgrounds become unavoidable.
